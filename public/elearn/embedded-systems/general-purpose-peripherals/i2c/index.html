01
MORE DEVICES, FEWER WIRES

What is I²C?

?
VedCurious
My board has several sensors. Do I need a separate data wire for every sensor?
✧
ParthExplaining
Not with I²C (Inter-Integrated Circuit). It lets a controller and multiple target devices share two signals: SDA for data and SCL for the clock.
!
VedSurprised
Just two wires for all of them? How does a device know I’m talking to it?
♥
ParthEncouraging
Each target has an address. The controller sends the address first, and the matching device responds.
QUICK CHECKWhich I²C signal carries the clock: SDA or SCL?
Think it through, then reveal the answer

SCL carries the clock; SDA carries the data.

02
OPEN-DRAIN LINES NEED A LIFT

Why are pull-up resistors needed?

?
VedConfused
I connected SDA and SCL, but the bus doesn’t work. What are those pull-up resistors for?
↗
ParthDemonstrating
I²C devices generally pull the lines low and release them; they don’t drive the shared bus high directly. Pull-up resistors bring SDA and SCL high when nobody is pulling them down.
…
VedThinking
Like several people can safely pull a rope down, but nobody forces it up against the others?
✦
ParthHappy
That’s a helpful picture. The resistor restores the high state. Its value and the bus capacitance affect rise time and signal quality.
QUICK CHECKWhat do the pull-ups do when every device releases SDA?
Think it through, then reveal the answer

They bring SDA to its high idle level, allowing the bus to represent a logic-high state.

03
START, ADDRESS, ACK

How does a transfer work?

?
VedCurious
What happens when the controller wants to read a sensor?
✧
ParthExplaining
The controller creates a START, sends the target address and a read/write direction bit, then checks for an ACK. Data bytes follow, each with an acknowledge bit, and the controller finishes with a STOP.
…
VedThinking
And NACK means the device didn’t answer?
↗
ParthDemonstrating
It means no acknowledge was received for that byte. The reason could be an address mismatch, a device that is busy or absent, or a transaction-ending condition. Firmware should handle it.
CAN YOU GUESS?If the address is wrong, which device should acknowledge the address?
Think it through, then reveal the answer

Normally none of the targets with a different address will acknowledge it; the controller will observe a NACK.

04
FIND THE RIGHT TARGET

What should you know about addresses?

?
VedCurious
Can I attach any number of devices to the bus?
✧
ParthExplaining
Addressing helps multiple targets share the bus, but the practical limit is set by more than the address space. Bus capacitance, speed, wiring, and device loading matter too.
?
VedConfused
What if two chips use the same address?
✓
ParthConfident
They may both respond, which can cause confusing results. Check for address pins, alternate addresses, or a bus switch when addresses conflict. Also make sure all devices support the chosen bus speed and voltage.
✦
Key takeaway

I²C saves pins and supports addressed targets, but pull-ups, capacitance, compatible voltage, speed, and unique addresses all need attention.

05
SMALL CHIPS, SHARED BUS

Where is I²C useful?

✦
VedExcited
Which kinds of chips commonly use it?
✦
ParthHappy
Small sensors, real-time clocks, configuration memories, and GPIO expanders are common examples. It is especially convenient when low pin count matters.
…
VedThinking
So if I need high-speed data to one nearby chip, I might choose SPI instead?
♥
ParthEncouraging
That can be a sensible comparison. Choose from the data rate, number of devices, wiring budget, and what the parts support.
✦
Key takeaway

I²C is a useful short-distance shared bus when a small number of wires and addressed targets are valuable.
